# ⏳ Your biggest asset is time

[Chapter 1](新手篇/01-你適合主動定被動投資.md) explained why long-term investing works. But the words "long term" carry a weight that words cannot convey — you have to see the numbers.

Three charts and one simulator, all making a single point:

> **When you start matters far more than how much you put in each month.**

---

## A pays in for ten years, B pays in for twenty

Two people invest $375 a month. They buy exactly the same thing and earn exactly the same returns.

Only one thing differs: **A pays in from year 1 to year 10, then never adds another cent.** **B does not start until year 11, but then pays in every month for twenty years.** B puts in $90,000; A puts in $45,000 — B contributes twice as much.

After thirty years:

The first ten years are only **33%** of what you pay in, but **57%** of what you end up with.

That is what "start early" actually means: the decade you strain hardest is not necessarily the decade that does the most work — **the decade that does the most work is the earliest one.**

!!! note "How the three charts above are calculated"
    $375 a month, a flat 7% a year, compounded monthly, contributions made at the start of each month, before fees and taxes, and assuming returns are **identical every year**.

    That is deliberate: it strips the idea down to its cleanest form. No real market ever hands you a punctual 7% — which is what the rest of this page is about.

---

## But the market will not politely hand you 7% every year

The real world looks like this: some years are +30%, some are −40%, and you have no way of knowing in advance which year is which. So "7% on average" quietly misleads you — you never live the average, you only ever live your own path.

The fix is not to find a better average. It is to **stop using averages**: run thousands of lives that could have happened, and look at the best, the worst, and the middle.

That is what the simulator below does. It is not a decoration — it draws monthly returns from the Dimson-Marsh-Staunton database, 126 years of real returns and volatility from 1900 to 2025, and when it finishes it re-runs the whole thing along **exactly the same market path** for someone who started ten years later, so you can compare. Every assumption is on the table, and you can change any of them.

## So

You cannot control returns. You cannot control when the crash arrives. You may not even control how much your pay goes up.

But **when you start** is yours to control — and of everything above, it is the variable that moves the result most.

> Time is the one thing you cannot make up later.

If you have not started: just read from [Chapter 1](新手篇/01-你適合主動定被動投資.md) onwards — you do not have to finish the site before you begin.
