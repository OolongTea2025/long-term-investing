    URLs change and articles get taken down. The links below worked when this was compiled and may not later. **If one is dead, searching the book or paper title will usually find it again.** What matters on this page is *which book, which study* — not the URL itself.

## 3. Useful tools

| Tool | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Portfolio Visualizer** (portfoliovisualizer.com) | Backtesting portfolios, factor analysis, Monte Carlo retirement simulation. |
| **Lazy Portfolio ETF** (lazyportfolioetf.com) | Historical rolling returns for various lazy portfolios (60/40 and others). |
| **Ken French Data Library** | Free download of academic-grade factor data. |
| **Bogleheads Forum** (bogleheads.org/forum) | The English-language passive investing community; searching past discussions is very useful. |

!!! warning "The right attitude toward these tools"
    Backtesting tools are excellent, but **past performance doesn't guarantee the future**, and it's very easy to pick the parameters that make the chart look the way you want (= overfitting). Treat them as **tools for understanding**, not as **crystal balls**.

## 🎉 You've read the whole site

From "active or passive" all the way through allocation, risk, factors, retirement, hard theory, and now this page. **What you know now puts you ahead of the large majority of people in the market.**

!!! tip "Three parting sentences — these are enough"
    1. **Buy the whole world (a total-market ETF like VT), on a fixed schedule, held long term.**
    2. **Don't time, don't chase what's hot, don't use leverage (the home you live in excepted).**
    3. **Real risk is permanent loss, not short-term volatility — choose a portfolio you can hold through a downturn.**

    Spend the rest of your time earning, and looking after your health and the people around you. **Leave the investing to the market and to compounding.**
